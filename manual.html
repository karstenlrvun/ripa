Guide

Everything you need to get started — nothing you don't.

Get it onto your phone

This app lives in your browser, but it works best set up like a regular app on your Home Screen.

On iPhone or iPad
  1. Open this page in Safari (it has to be Safari, not Chrome or another browser).
  2. Tap the Share button (the square with an arrow pointing up).
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. Tap Add in the top corner.

An icon appears on your Home Screen. Always open the app from that icon, not from a browser tab — Safari can quietly clear a browser tab's saved data after a week of not visiting it, but an installed icon is safe from that.

On a Mac
  1. Open this page in Safari.
  2. From the menu bar choose File → Add to Dock.

If you study more than one language here, add each one separately — they're separate pages with separate progress.

Studying

Tap Study, then tap the card to reveal the answer. Rate how it went:

Again you didn't get it — Good you got it — Easy you knew it instantly

The app decides when to show you each word again based on how you rate it. Words you find easy come back less often; words you struggle with come back sooner. You don't need to manage this yourself — just answer honestly.

Each word can appear in two directions — for example Latin→English and English→Latin. They're tracked separately, so knowing one direction doesn't automatically mean you know the other.

Settings, in brief

New words a day — how many unfamiliar words get introduced each day.

Target retention — how often you want to succeed when a word comes up. Higher means more review, but less forgetting.

A study day begins at — when your daily count resets, for people who study past midnight.

Unlock the reverse direction after — a delay before a word's reverse direction shows up, so you meet it one way first.

Weekly review day — nudges some reviews onto a day that suits you, like a Sunday catch-up.

Theme — pick a look, light or dark, and whether it changes daily.

Wide-screen study — on a tablet, laptop, or a sideways phone, three optional extras: review history on the card, keyboard-shortcut labels, and a bit of motion when you reveal or move on.

Keeping your progress in step across devices

Open Settings and tap Get a code for this device. You'll get a short code like AJYJQ-FLFB8. Open the same page on your other device, go to Settings, and type that same code into the Sync code box. From then on, tapping Sync now on either device keeps them both up to date, and it also happens quietly on its own a few seconds after each answer you rate.

The same code works across every Riverbank deck too, not just one language — type it into Latin, Greek, Italian, and SAT's Settings alike, and each keeps its own progress in step everywhere you use it.

There's no account to create and nothing to remember except that one code — write it down somewhere safe.

If you studied on both devices before syncing

Normally syncing is invisible — you study, it saves, the other device picks it up next time you open it there. There's one situation where the app stops and asks you something: if you study on your phone, then separately study on your laptop before the phone ever gets a chance to sync, both devices now have answers the other doesn't know about.

When that happens, the next sync shows you a message naming both devices and how many new answers each has — for example, "This device (iPhone) has 3 reviews the synced copy does not have yet. The synced copy was last pushed by Laptop and has 5 reviews this device does not have." You're asked to choose:

  • OK takes the other device's copy. This device's new answers since the last sync are lost, unless you export a backup first (Settings → Export a backup file).
  • Cancel changes nothing and asks again next time. Use this if you want to think about it, or if you'd rather go to the other device and tap Sync now there instead, which keeps this device's copy going forward.

The app can't combine the two histories into one — it can only pick one copy or the other. Syncing after most study sessions, rather than saving it up, is the easiest way to avoid ever seeing this message at all.

If something goes wrong

Export a backup file in Settings saves everything to a file you can keep somewhere safe, like iCloud Drive or email it to yourself.

Restore from a file loads a backup file back in.

Erase all progress saves a backup automatically, then wipes this device clean — useful if you want a fresh start.
